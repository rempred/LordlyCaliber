COMBAT SYSTEMS

Damage

How attack power, defense, fatigue, and Poison affect HP loss.

Damage is the HP a character loses from an attack. The game starts with the attacker’s power, subtracts the defender’s defense, then applies Luck, elemental resistance, and random variation.

The damage formula

Physical attacks and damaging magic share this ordinary calculation. Click any linked term to see what it means.

ORDINARY SUCCESSFUL ATTACK
Damage = (Attack power − Defense + Luck difference) × (100 − Elemental resistance) 100 × Random multiplier

Discard the decimal part. Limit the ordinary result to 1–9,999.

Defense removes an amount. Resistance then reduces what remains by a percentage. This order is why a 20% loss of defense does not always mean 20% more damage.

Attack power and defense

Attack power and Defense are calculated values. They combine a character’s class, current attributes, equipment, and battle conditions.

Physical attacks use physical attack power against physical defense. Damaging magic uses magic attack power against magic defense.

Class coefficients and editor names
Class coefficient Short name Used to calculate
Physical Attack Coefficient PAtk Coef Physical attack power
Magic Attack Coefficient MAtk Coef Magic attack power
Physical Defense Coefficient PDef Coef Physical defense
Magic Defense Coefficient MDef Coef Magic defense

A class coefficient is one input to the formula, not the completed attack power or defense. It is separate from the class’s starting attributes and gains per level.

Physical attack power

Physical attack power = Physical Attack Coefficient + Weapon STR bonus 6
× STR + Nonweapon equipment STR bonuses + 50 50
× DEX + Equipment DEX bonuses + 100 100
× Attack condition multiplier

Discard the decimal part after the complete calculation.

Physical defense

Physical defense = Physical Defense Coefficient 20
× VIT + Equipment VIT bonuses + 25 25
× STR + Equipment STR bonuses + 100 100
× Defense condition multiplier

Discard the decimal part after the complete calculation.

STR contributes to both physical attack power and physical defense. DEX helps physical attack power; VIT helps physical defense.

Magic attack power

Magic attack power = Magic Attack Coefficient + Equipment INT bonuses 6
× INT + 50 50
× MEN + Equipment MEN bonuses + 100 100
× Magic attack condition multiplier

Discard the decimal part after the complete calculation.

Magic defense

Magic defense = Magic Defense Coefficient 20
× MEN + Equipment MEN bonuses + 25 25
× INT + Equipment INT bonuses + 100 100
× Defense condition multiplier

Discard the decimal part after the complete calculation.

INT and MEN each contribute to both magic attack power and magic defense.

Use the character’s current attributes.

Level gains and temporary effects such as Poison change the attributes used here. Equipment bonuses are then added where the formulas show them.

A level-1 Amazon has a Physical Defense Coefficient of 20, STR 50, and VIT 45. Her default Short Bow adds 3 STR. With a condition multiplier of 1.00, physical defense is 4. The worked example shows each step.

The resulting attack power can receive further adjustments for a particular action or target. That adjusted value enters the main damage formula.

Luck, resistance, and variation

Luck difference

The attacker’s total combat Luck is compared with the defender’s. Higher Luck adds to damage before resistance and random variation apply.

Luck difference =
(Attacker’s Luck + Attacker’s equipment Luck)
− (Defender’s Luck + Defender’s equipment Luck)

A difference of +5 adds 5 at this stage. A difference of −5 subtracts 5. Permanent Luck gains from earlier levels are already part of the character’s Luck.

Elemental resistance

Elemental resistance applies after defense. The game uses the defender’s resistance to the attack’s element, including when a physical attack uses an element.

How resistance changes the remaining damage
Resistance Amount retained
0 100%
20 80%
50 50%
80 20%

Resistance is limited to 0–100. At 100, the multiplication produces zero, then the ordinary calculation applies its minimum result of 1. Special effects and failed success checks have separate rules.

Random variation

The random multiplier ranges from 0.90 to 1.10. It changes the amount by up to 10% in either direction before the decimal part is discarded. The possible outcomes are not necessarily equally frequent.

Worked example: Fighter against Amazon

A level-1 Fighter hits a level-1 Amazon with a Short Sword. At a random multiplier of 1.00, the hit deals 10 HP damage. Here is how the game reaches that result.

This calculated matchup uses the US retail class table’s level-1 attributes and default equipment. Your characters can have different attributes because of level gains and class changes.

The Fighter stands in the front row. The Amazon stands in the rear row. Both units fight on Plain terrain with fatigue 0. Neither character has Poison or another temporary effect.

A weapon’s STR bonus has two different uses.

The Fighter’s Short Sword adds 3 beside his class attack coefficient. The Amazon’s Short Bow adds 3 to the STR factor in her defense. Do not add the sword’s 3 twice when calculating the Fighter’s attack power.

3. Calculate the battle conditions

Plain terrain gives both characters a terrain adjustment of zero. The Fighter’s front-row physical attack adds 5 percentage points. The Amazon’s formation row does not add to her defense.

Neither character has a fatigue penalty, a typed Spellbook, or an applicable class or equipment condition bonus. Thus, the only condition adjustment is the Fighter’s +5.

Attack condition multiplier = (100 + 5 − 0) ÷ 100 = 1.05

Defense condition multiplier = (100 + 0 − 0) ÷ 100 = 1.00

6. Calculate Luck and resistance

Both characters have Luck 50, and their equipment adds no Luck. The Luck difference is therefore (50 + 0) − (50 + 0) = 0.

The Short Sword deals Physical damage. Use the Amazon’s Physical resistance, including her armor and Bandanna:

Physical resistance = 48 + 3 + 1 = 52

Amount retained = (100 − 52) ÷ 100 = 0.48

The class supplies 48, Leather Armor supplies 3, and Bandanna supplies 1. Thus, 48% of the amount left after defense and Luck reaches the random-variation step.

Fatigue

Fatigue weakens physical and magic attack power, both forms of defense, accuracy, and evasion. The first penalty begins at fatigue 70.

Fatigue penalty thresholds
Fatigue Penalty Multiplier*
0–69 0 1.00
70–79 5 0.95
80–89 18 0.82
90 or more 40 0.60

*When the other battle condition bonuses total zero.

The attacker and defender use their own fatigue. The penalty enters the condition multiplier alongside the applicable battle bonuses.

Attack condition multiplier = 100 + Physical attack condition bonuses − Fatigue penalty 100
Magic attack condition multiplier = 100 + Magic attack condition bonuses − Fatigue penalty 100
Defense condition multiplier = 100 + Defense condition bonuses − Fatigue penalty 100

Battle bonuses include terrain and movement interactions, certain equipment or class interactions, and formation for attacks. Magic attack conditions also include a weapon and attack-element interaction.

At fatigue 90 with other bonuses of +20, the multiplier is (100 + 20 − 40) ÷ 100 = 0.80. Fatigue does not simply multiply every completed value by 0.60.

Example: the Amazon becomes tired

Return to the Fighter against Amazon example. Keep the Fighter fresh, attack power 26, resistance 52, equal Luck, and random multiplier 1.00.

The Amazon’s defense before its condition multiplier is about 4.284. Apply fatigue to this value before discarding decimals.

Fatigue changes the Amazon's defense and damage taken
Amazon’s fatigue Defense calculation Defense Damage calculation
0–69 4.284 × 1.00 → 4 4 (26 − 4) × 0.48 → 10 HP
70–79 4.284 × 0.95 → 4 4 (26 − 4) × 0.48 → 10 HP
80–89 4.284 × 0.82 → 3 3 (26 − 3) × 0.48 → 11 HP
90 or more 4.284 × 0.60 → 2 2 (26 − 2) × 0.48 → 11 HP

At fatigue 70, her defense calculation decreases, but it still becomes 4 after rounding. Thus, this particular hit still deals 10 HP. At fatigue 80, defense becomes 3 and the hit deals 11 HP.

What if the Fighter is tired?

At fatigue 90, his front-row condition multiplier is (100 + 5 − 40) ÷ 100 = 0.65. His attack power becomes 25.6167 × 0.65 ≈ 16.6508, then 16.

Against the fresh Amazon, damage is (16 − 4) × 0.48 = 5.76, then 5 HP. If both characters have fatigue 90, damage is (16 − 2) × 0.48 = 6.72, then 6 HP.

Reducing fatigue

Power Fruit removes 20 fatigue. Angel Fruit removes 50. Neither can reduce fatigue below zero. Soldiers start battle with no fatigue penalty.

Poison Cloud

Poison Cloud is the Earth spell in the standard T3 group used by Witches. T3 means “Elemental Tier 3 Spell Template”; it is a spell group, not a character level.

Its weakness effect reduces all seven attributes: STR, VIT, INT, MEN, AGI, DEX, and Luck. With no other temporary attribute adjustment:

Attribute while poisoned = Original attribute × 0.90

Discard the decimal part. This version assumes no other temporary attribute adjustment.

An attribute of 100 becomes 90; an attribute of 99 becomes 89. These weakened attributes feed the attack power, defense, accuracy, and evasion calculations. Separate equipment bonuses are added later.

Example: the Amazon has Poison

Use the same Fighter and Amazon, both with fatigue 0. Poison changes the Amazon’s STR from 50 to 45, VIT from 45 to 40, and Luck from 50 to 45.

The VIT calculation is 45 × 0.90 = 40.5, which becomes 40. Her Short Bow still adds 3 STR. Her equipment and class still give Physical resistance 52.

Physical defense = (20 ÷ 20) × ((40 + 0 + 25) ÷ 25) × ((45 + 3 + 100) ÷ 100) × 1.00

≈ 3.848 → 3 defense

Luck difference = 50 − 45 = +5

Damage = (26 − 3 + 5) × 0.48 × 1.00 = 13.44 → 13 HP

The same hit now deals 13 HP instead of 10. Poison reduced both defense and Luck; the extra damage is not simply a 10% increase.

If the poisoned Amazon also has fatigue 90, her defense becomes 3.848 × 0.60 = 2.3088, then 2. Damage becomes (26 − 2 + 5) × 0.48 = 13.92, then 13 HP. Rounding can hide a further change for one particular hit.

Other temporary effects

Poison subtracts ten percentage points from the temporary attribute adjustment. A separate +10% adjustment and Poison’s −10% weakness cancel each other.

Attribute while poisoned =
Original attribute + Original attribute
× (Other temporary attribute adjustment − 0.10)

Multiply the adjustment by the original attribute, then add the original attribute. Discard the decimal part.

The calculation always starts from the original attributes. Reapplying Poison does not keep multiplying an already weakened attribute by 0.90.

Poison and fatigue can apply together.

Poison reduces attributes. Fatigue reduces condition multipliers. Poison does not raise the fatigue value or directly reduce equipment bonuses, class coefficients, or elemental resistance.

Accuracy and evasion

Accuracy and Evasion feed a separate success check. Higher accuracy helps the attacker pass it; higher evasion makes success less likely.

Both use AGI and DEX, equipment, fatigue, and applicable battle conditions. Poison also weakens them by reducing AGI and DEX.

Defense reduces the damage amount. Evasion affects the success check. A character can have high defense and low evasion.

A failed success check does not always mean zero damage. Some damaging magic can deal half the calculated amount. Status effects and special attacks have their own success rules.

Double damage and special attacks

Basic physical attacks and Shoot have a separate Luck-based check that can double calculated damage. It uses the characters’ Luck attributes, without the separate equipment Luck bonuses used in the damage formula.

Some special attacks scale attack power, scale damage, or calculate an amount from current HP. Combined spells can also change how power is shared across targets. The ordinary formula does not describe every special attack or status effect.

Attack counts depend on class and formation row. FrontAtks, MidAtks, and RearAtks give the number of attacks from each row, not the damage per attack.

Battle glossary

These are the same definitions that open from the formula links.

The damage calculation

Damage

The calculated HP loss from an attack. Current HP stops at zero.

Attack power

The strength calculated for an attack before defense, Luck, elemental resistance, and random variation apply. Physical attacks and magic use different attributes.

Defense

The calculated amount subtracted from attack power. Physical attacks use physical defense; magic uses magic defense.

Luck difference

The attacker’s Luck and equipment Luck bonuses, minus the defender’s equivalent total. The result can be positive or negative.

Elemental resistance

The defender’s percentage protection against the attack’s element. It reduces the amount left after defense and Luck.

Random multiplier

A factor from 0.90 through 1.10 that varies the ordinary damage result. The possible outcomes are not necessarily equally frequent.

Discard the decimal part

Keep the whole-number part of the result. The game does this after calculating each complete attack power or defense value and after the ordinary damage multiplication.

The 1–9,999 limit

The ordinary damage result cannot be lower than 1 or higher than 9,999. This bound belongs to the ordinary calculation. Special effects, double damage, and failed success checks have separate rules.

Attributes

STR — Strength

Contributes to ordinary physical attack power and physical defense. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

VIT — Vitality

Contributes to physical defense. Some special attacks can also use it. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

INT — Intelligence

Contributes to magic attack power and magic defense. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

MEN — Mentality

Contributes to magic attack power and magic defense. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

AGI — Agility

Contributes to accuracy and evasion. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

DEX — Dexterity

Contributes to ordinary physical attack power, accuracy, and evasion. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

LCK — Luck

Contributes to ordinary damage and separate Luck-dependent checks. Use the current combat attribute, before adding separate equipment bonuses. Poison’s weakness applies to this attribute.

Class coefficients

Class coefficient

One of four fixed class values used to calculate physical or magic attack power and defense. Class coefficients are separate from base attributes, growth per level, and attack counts.

Physical Attack Coefficient

A fixed class value that enters the physical attack power formula. The editor labels it PAtk Coef. It is neither completed attack power nor a percentage.

Magic Attack Coefficient

A fixed class value that enters the magic attack power formula. The editor labels it MAtk Coef. Equipment INT bonuses are added to it in the first part of the formula.

Physical Defense Coefficient

A fixed class value that enters the physical defense formula. The editor labels it PDef Coef. Divide it by 20 before applying the attribute and condition factors.

Magic Defense Coefficient

A fixed class value that enters the magic defense formula. The editor labels it MDef Coef. Divide it by 20 before applying the attribute and condition factors.

Equipment and battle conditions

Weapon STR bonus

The STR bonus from the first recognized weapon in equipment-slot order. It enters the first part of the ordinary physical attack power formula. With no weapon, this term is zero.

Nonweapon equipment STR bonuses

The combined STR bonuses from equipped items that are not weapons. The physical attack formula uses this term separately from the weapon’s STR bonus.

Equipment STR bonuses

The sum of the Strength bonuses from all four equipped items. Add these bonuses where the formula shows them. Poison does not directly reduce the bonuses themselves.

Equipment DEX bonuses

The sum of the Dexterity bonuses from all four equipped items. Add these bonuses where the formula shows them. Poison does not directly reduce the bonuses themselves.

Equipment INT bonuses

The sum of the Intelligence bonuses from all four equipped items. Add these bonuses where the formula shows them. Poison does not directly reduce the bonuses themselves.

Equipment MEN bonuses

The sum of the Mentality bonuses from all four equipped items. Add these bonuses where the formula shows them. Poison does not directly reduce the bonuses themselves.

Equipment VIT bonuses

The sum of the Vitality bonuses from all four equipped items. Add these bonuses where the formula shows them. Poison does not directly reduce the bonuses themselves.

Equipment AGI bonuses

The sum of the Agility bonuses from all four equipped items. Add these bonuses where the formula shows them. Poison does not directly reduce the bonuses themselves.

Equipment Luck bonuses

The combined combat Luck bonuses from the four equipped items. These are added to the character’s Luck for ordinary damage. They are separate from permanent Luck gains received at a level increase.

Attack condition multiplier

The physical attack factor for battle conditions. Add the applicable bonuses, subtract the attacker’s fatigue penalty, add 100, then divide by 100.

Magic attack condition multiplier

The attack condition factor for magic. It includes the physical attack condition components plus the weapon and attack-element interaction. It uses the attacker’s fatigue.

Defense condition multiplier

The condition factor used by both physical and magic defense. It uses the defender’s applicable battle bonuses and fatigue penalty. Formation is not part of this defense factor.

Status and success

Fatigue

A character’s fatigue value determines a penalty to attack power, defense, accuracy, and evasion. The first penalty begins at 70. Poison does not increase this value.

Fatigue penalty

Subtract 0 for fatigue below 70, 5 for 70–79, 18 for 80–89, or 40 for 90 and above. The attacker and defender use their own fatigue.

Poison Cloud

The Earth spell in the standard T3 group used by Witches. Its weakness effect reduces STR, VIT, INT, MEN, AGI, DEX, and Luck by 10% when no other temporary attribute adjustment applies.

Accuracy

The attacker’s calculated value for an attack’s success check. AGI, DEX, equipment, fatigue, and applicable battle conditions affect it. Higher accuracy helps the attack succeed.

Evasion

The defender’s calculated value for the success check. Higher evasion makes success less likely. It is separate from defense, which reduces the damage amount.

Formula factors and constants

100: the percentage scale

A full amount is 100%. Dividing by 100 converts a percentage into a multiplier.

Physical attack power

The attack power used by ordinary physical attacks. It combines the class’s Physical Attack Coefficient, STR, DEX, equipment, and attack conditions.

Magic attack power

The attack power used by ordinary damaging magic. It combines the class’s Magic Attack Coefficient, INT, MEN, equipment, and magic attack conditions.

Physical defense

The amount subtracted from a physical attack’s power. It combines the class’s Physical Defense Coefficient, VIT, STR, equipment, and defense conditions.

Magic defense

The amount subtracted from a magic attack’s power. It combines the class’s Magic Defense Coefficient, MEN, INT, equipment, and defense conditions.

Attacker’s Luck

The attacking character’s current LCK attribute before separate equipment Luck bonuses. Permanent gains from previous levels are already included.

Defender’s Luck

The defending character’s current LCK attribute before separate equipment Luck bonuses. This value and the defender’s equipment Luck are subtracted in the Luck difference.

Physical attack condition bonuses

The sum of applicable terrain and movement, Spellbook or Alignment, formation, anti-dragon, and same-side leader or monster-affinity bonuses. Each component can depend on the combatants and attack.

Magic attack condition bonuses

The physical attack condition bonuses plus the interaction between the weapon and the attack’s element. Add the components before applying the condition multiplier.

Defense condition bonuses

The defender’s terrain and movement, Spellbook or Alignment, anti-dragon, and same-side leader or monster-affinity terms. Some terrain conditions can add a further bonus. Formation and weapon/attack-element bonuses are excluded.

Original attribute

The character’s attribute before the current temporary attribute adjustments. Use the character’s value after level gains, not the class’s starting value.

Attribute while poisoned

The combat attribute after Poison’s weakness effect. With no other temporary adjustment, take 90% of the original attribute and discard the decimal part.

Other temporary attribute adjustment

The total of temporary attribute changes apart from Poison. Express it as a decimal in the full weakness formula: +10% is +0.10. Poison subtracts 0.10 from this adjustment.

0.10: Poison’s reduction

Poison subtracts ten percentage points from the temporary attribute adjustment. The game recalculates from the original attribute, so repeated applications do not keep multiplying the weakened value by 0.90.

0.90: the amount retained

With Poison alone, the character keeps 90% of each original attribute. Separate equipment bonuses are added later.

6: attack scaling

A fixed divisor in both ordinary attack power formulas. Divide the combined class coefficient and specified equipment bonus by 6 before multiplying by the other factors.

20: defense scaling

A fixed divisor in both defense formulas. Divide the class defense coefficient by 20 before multiplying by the attribute and condition factors.

50: the attack attribute scale

A fixed number used in the leading attribute factor for ordinary attack power. Add 50 to the indicated attribute and bonuses, then divide by 50.

100: the secondary attribute scale

A fixed number used in the second attribute factor. Add 100 to the indicated attribute and equipment bonuses, then divide by 100.

25: the defense attribute scale

A fixed number used in the leading defense attribute factor. Add 25 to VIT or MEN and its equipment bonuses, then divide by 25.

COMBAT SYSTEMS

Accuracy & evasion

Why attacks hit, why they fail, and why the game shows GUARD! or MISS!

The main rules

This guide explains the ordinary physical-hit check in the US retail version of Ogre Battle 64.

  • AGI, DEX, and Luck all affect the chance to avoid a normal physical attack.
  • The attacker and defender both matter. A character has no fixed dodge percentage against every opponent.
  • GUARD! and MISS! can show the same failed hit check. The defender’s class selects the label.
  • The label selection makes no second guard or dodge roll.
  • Fatigue can reduce accuracy and evasion. Equipment and some battle conditions also affect them.

Start with the explanations and example. Use the full formulas when you want to calculate a result.

GUARD! and MISS!

When this physical-hit check fails, the defender avoids the attack. The game then selects a message from the defender’s class setting.

MessageExample defender classes
GUARD!Fighter, Knight, Ninja, Sword Master
MISS!Amazon, Wizard, Archer, Ghost

A Ninja can attack a Ghost and get MISS!. The same Ninja can attack a Sword Master and get GUARD!.

The different words do not show which defender has better evasion. Neither word proves that another defensive roll occurred.

This guide uses avoid the attack for either result. A separate DODGE message is not verified for this path.

Which attributes help

The attacker performs the attack. The defender is its target.

AttributeWhen this character attacksWhen this character defends
AGI: AgilityRaises accuracyRaises evasion
DEX: DexterityRaises accuracyRaises evasion
LCK: LuckRaises the hit thresholdLowers the attacker’s hit threshold

Accuracy and evasion are calculated scores. Neither score is a percentage.

More DEX can therefore help a defender avoid an attack. AGI is not the only evasion attribute.

The gain depends on both characters, their conditions, and rounding. Ten extra points in an attribute do not promise ten percentage points of improvement.

Defense reduces the damage calculation. Evasion affects the hit check. A character can have high defense and low evasion.

Use current attributes and add equipment once

  1. Start with the character’s current combat AGI, DEX, and Luck.
  2. Use any changes from Poison or another temporary attribute effect.
  3. Add the separate equipment bonuses shown in each formula.

Add together the relevant bonuses from all four equipped items. If your supplied values already include those bonuses, do not add them again.

How the hit check works

  1. Calculate the attacker’s accuracy.
  2. Calculate the defender’s evasion.
  3. Use both scores, the Luck difference, and the battle-side adjustment to calculate the hit threshold.
  4. Compare a roll from 0 through 99 with that threshold.
Roll below the hit threshold: the attack passes the hit check.
Roll equal to or above the threshold: the defender avoids the attack.

For example, a threshold of 75 accepts rolls 0 through 74. Rolls 75 through 99 fail.

Hit thresholdAccepted roll valuesHit percentageAvoid percentage
0None0%100%
750 through 7475%25%
1000 through 99100%0%

These percentages assume that all 100 roll values are equally likely. They describe a chance, not a promised result over 100 attacks.

There is no completed hit followed by another dodge roll in this path. The comparison above decides whether this hit check passes.

A separate double-damage check can change damage after a successful check. It does not choose between GUARD! and MISS!.

Full formulas

These formulas apply when the ordinary physical-hit calculation is used. Read the special cases below before applying them to other attacks.

Truncate means discard the decimal part. Keep fractions during each calculation and truncate only at the marked steps.

1. Attacker accuracy

Accuracy = truncate(
    (attacker DEX + attacker equipment DEX + 50)
    × ((attacker AGI + attacker equipment AGI + 100) / 55)
    × ((100 + attacker leader bonus − attacker fatigue penalty) / 100)
)

2. Defender evasion

Evasion = truncate(
    (defender AGI + defender equipment AGI + 50)
    × ((defender DEX + defender equipment DEX + 100) / 50)
    × ((100 + defender terrain adjustment
            + defender leader bonus − defender fatigue penalty) / 100)
)

3. Hit threshold

Luck difference =
    attacker Luck + attacker equipment Luck
    − defender Luck − defender equipment Luck

Hit threshold = limit to 0–100(
    truncate((Accuracy / Evasion) × (100 + Luck difference))
    + battle-side adjustment
)

Limit to 0–100 means replace results below 0 with 0, and results above 100 with 100.

4. Simple percentages

Hit percentage   = Hit threshold
Avoid percentage = 100 − Hit threshold

These percentages count accepted and rejected roll values. They do not measure a separate dodge roll.

The game uses limited numerical precision. A hand calculation near a rounding boundary can differ from the game.

The battle-side adjustment

The battle-side adjustment is 0 or +10. Add it after truncating the score-and-Luck result, then apply the 0–100 limit.

Its assignment to the player or enemy is not confirmed for every battle setup. Do not assume that the player always receives +10.

This is a separate term from the class leader affinity bonus.

What +10 and +15 mean

With no fatigue or terrain adjustment, +10 gives a score multiplier of 1.10. A bonus of +15 gives 1.15.

These values increase the applicable accuracy or evasion score. They do not directly add 10 or 15 percentage points to the final chance.

For example, Beast Tamer gives a qualifying Wyrm +10. Beast Master gives that Wyrm +15.

Guide glossary

Attacker

The character that performs the attack.

Defender

The character targeted by the attack.

AGI: Agility

A character attribute that contributes to both accuracy and evasion.

DEX: Dexterity

A character attribute that contributes to both accuracy and evasion. It also contributes to ordinary physical attack power.

LCK: Luck

The attacker’s Luck and the defender’s Luck affect the hit threshold. Add each character’s separate equipment Luck bonuses once.

Accuracy

The attacker’s calculated score for the hit check. It is not a percentage.

Example: Accuracy 545 must still be compared with the defender’s evasion.

Evasion

The defender’s calculated score for the hit check. A higher score makes an ordinary physical hit less likely.

Hit threshold

A whole number from 0 through 100. The attack passes the check when its random roll is below this number.

Example: A threshold of 75 accepts rolls 0 through 74.

Roll

The final random whole number used for this comparison. It ranges from 0 through 99.

Example: At a threshold of 75, roll 74 passes and roll 75 fails.

Percentage in this guide

The share of the 100 possible roll values that gives a result. This assumes that each roll value is equally likely.

Example: A threshold of 75 accepts 75 roll values and rejects 25. It does not guarantee 75 hits in every 100 attacks.

Equipment bonus

The sum of the relevant bonuses from all four equipped items. Add AGI bonuses to AGI, DEX bonuses to DEX, and Luck bonuses to Luck.

Class leader affinity bonus

A bonus for certain leader and member classes on the same battle side. Use the class leader affinity table. The bonus enters both accuracy and evasion.

Example: An applicable +15 bonus adds 15 inside the score’s condition calculation. It does not guarantee 15 more percentage points of success.

Terrain adjustment

A value from −5 through +3, selected from the character’s movement type and the battle terrain. It enters evasion in this calculation.

Battle-side adjustment

The game adds either 0 or 10 to the attacker’s hit threshold before the 0–100 limit. Its player/enemy assignment is not confirmed for every battle setup.

Truncate: discard the decimal part

Keep only the whole-number part. Do this at the marked steps.

Example: 68.9 becomes 68. A value of −3.9 becomes −3.

Limit to 0–100

Use 0 for a result below 0. Use 100 for a result above 100. Keep other results unchanged.

COMBAT SYSTEMS

Unity & combined spells

Choose the right spells. Bring the casters together. Give the combination a chance.

Unity helps a squad cast combined spells. Two or three casters can join their spells into one result. The recipe determines what they cast and which positions it can affect.

High Unity improves the chance, not the damage.

Unity 99 always passes the Unity check. The casters must still be ready, share a target, and have a valid recipe.

Set up a combination

  1. Choose a combination from the recipe list.
  2. Use the class-row table to select the required spell groups.
  3. Equip spellbooks that produce the required elements.
  4. Use a tactic that directs the casters toward the same target.
  5. Keep the casters together as a starting formation, then check whether they act together.

All participating casters must be ready when the battle system groups them. A valid recipe alone does not guarantee a combination.

The Leader tactic has produced a combination in a recorded battle where Strongest split the casters’ targets. This is a useful setup choice, not a guarantee.

Same-line formations are a useful starting point. A complete position-by-position rule for visible formations is not yet established.

What Unity changes

Unity belongs to the squad, rather than to an individual caster or a particular pair. A successful combination does not spend it.

The game draws a whole-number roll from 0 through 99. The Unity check passes when:

Roll ≤ Unity

A roll equal to Unity passes.

The selected target position is the center of the spell’s area. It is not necessarily the center of the enemy formation.

Side positions are directly above, below, left, and right. Diagonal positions are the four corners. The game skips empty, out-of-bounds, or otherwise invalid positions.

Unity glossary

Click a linked term anywhere in this article to open the same explanation.

Unity

One value shared by a squad. It controls the chance of passing the combined-spell check. It does not increase spell power or get spent when a combination succeeds.

Unity check

The game draws an integer from 0 through 99. The check passes when that number is equal to or below the squad’s Unity. Other combination requirements still apply.

Combined spell

A spell formed from two or three eligible casters. Their actual spells must match a recipe, and their squad must pass its Unity check.

T1, T2, and T3

Short names for three spell groups in this guide. They are not character levels. A class’s formation-row action selects the group; equipment can select its element.

Actual spell

The spell a caster will use after class, formation row, and equipment are considered. The recipe checks this spell, not the caster’s class name.

Recipe

An exact set of compatible spells and its combined result. A spell’s presence in the compatible list does not mean it combines with every other listed spell.

Shared target

The final participating casters must select the same target. A tactic can affect their choices. Passing the Unity check does not make different target choices match.

Flag of Unity

An item that adds a random 1–5 Unity to a squad, up to 100. Normal retail shop lists do not sell it. A reliable normal-play acquisition route is not established.

Spellbook priority

For a standard elemental spell group, the first equipped spellbook selects the element before any weapon can. A plain Spellbook uses the caster’s assigned element.

Three participating casters

All three selected spells survive the recipe checks and contribute power. Three available casters do not always produce this result.

A third caster is left out

The game can select a compatible pair from three candidates. Only the retained pair contributes to the combined spell. Use the pair’s target area and power rules.

Selected target position

The position around which the spell’s target area is arranged. It receives the full sum of participating caster power. It need not be the formation’s center.

Side position

A position directly above, below, left, or right of the selected target position. Its incoming power depends on the pair or trio recipe.

Diagonal position

One of the four corner positions around the selected target position. Only a nine-position combined spell includes these positions.

A — highest caster power

The largest participating magic attack power for the target being evaluated. The game sorts the calculated powers; A is not a fixed formation slot.

B — second-highest caster power

The second-largest participating magic attack power for the current target. In a pair, this is the weaker of the two power values.

C — lowest caster power in a trio

The smallest participating magic attack power when three casters contribute. A caster dropped from a three-candidate selection contributes no C value.

Combined power for a target

The power passed into the ordinary magic calculation at one target position. It comes from the participating caster powers and the recipe’s area rule. It is not yet HP damage.

Source caster

The acting caster whose Luck enters the target’s ordinary effect calculation. Adding the participants’ attack powers does not add all their Luck together.
